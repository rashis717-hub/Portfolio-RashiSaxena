<Section n="01" title="Context & Business Problem" headline="Sales meetings produce talk, not signal">

### The enterprise landscape

A large sales organisation runs on meetings: weekly regional reviews, area reviews, product discussions, follow-ups. They are where plans are made, blockers are raised and commitments are given. But the organisation is also a hierarchy: a managing director, segment heads, regional and zonal managers, branch managers and field sales managers, each needing a different view of the same activity.

SalesPulze is an enterprise meeting intelligence and execution platform for sales organisations. It takes what happens in meetings and turns it into insight, actions and scores that each level can use.

### The core problem

- **Nobody sees the pattern.** Each meeting is a one-off. Whether a regional review is getting healthier, or the same blocker keeps returning, is invisible.
- **Actions disappear.** Commitments made in a meeting are not tracked in one place, so follow-through depends on memory.
- **One view does not fit every level.** A managing director needs the health of the whole field. A manager needs their own team and their next meeting.
- **AI summaries are hard to trust.** If a tool states that a meeting went badly, a manager needs to know where that came from and what to do if it is wrong.
- **Meetings have a cost.** Nobody sees what the time spent in meetings costs.

| What people experience | What it costs the business |
|---|---|
| Meetings are held and forgotten | Repeated blockers and slow follow-through |
| The same dashboard for everyone | Leaders buried in detail, managers missing what is theirs |
| AI outputs with no source | Mistrust, and the tool is ignored |
| Actions tracked in separate places | Missed commitments |
| No view of meeting cost | Time spent without being measured |

### How I researched

I worked out the structure of the organisation first, because everything in the product depends on it. I mapped the hierarchy from the top level to the field, and for each level I wrote down the decisions it makes and the information it needs. I then analysed the existing screens in Figma and produced an outline for an 11-chapter user guide, which doubled as an audit of what the product already did. {/* [YOUR INPUT: any customer conversations, workshops or other research you ran, and AI-assisted research if you used it] */}

### What each level needs

{/* [YOUR INPUT: confirm the hierarchy. The notes list five levels, while the Figma level selector shows Level 2 to Level 8 for a deeper customer hierarchy. Say which to show.] */}

| Level | The question they ask | What they need to see |
|---|---|---|
| Managing director and president | Where is the field healthy, and where is it not? | The whole field at a glance, with the weakest parts surfaced |
| Segment manager | Which products and regions are driving results? | Performance by segment and product coverage |
| Regional and zonal managers | Is my region executing well? | Their regions, their meetings and their open actions |
| Branch managers | Is my team following the process? | Hygiene, attendance and follow-through |
| Field sales managers | What do I need to do next? | Their next meeting, their actions and their blockers |

### What shaped the design

1. **The hierarchy is the interface.** The same hub should answer the right question for each level, by changing its scope and not its layout.
2. **A score must explain itself.** A number is only trusted if the person can see what drives it. Positive and negative drivers sit next to every score.
3. **AI must show its working.** Confidence, evidence and a way to flag a result are part of every AI output, not extras.

### Role-based views and edge cases

| Scenario | How the design handles it |
|---|---|
| A meeting is not mapped to a node | An Unassigned Meetings area where an administrator assigns it, with a preview and a log |
| A customer's hierarchy differs | A Scopes and Structure area that defines the structure, with a node-level scope selector in the hub |
| AI is not confident | A visible low-confidence label, and the evidence to check it |
| A result is wrong | A flag on the series, review, revert and an audit trail |
| Meeting names are inconsistent | Meeting name mapping with a preview, built from node, product, title or any text |
| Cost goes over a limit | Budget alerts in Cost Insights |
| A different brand per customer | Client logo and brand name, with attribution at the login boundary |

<Section n="05" title="Final Solutions" headline="The right view for each level, and a reason to trust every number">

<SubSection n="01" title="Intel Hub: the whole field at a glance">

**The problem it solves:** leaders cannot see where the field is healthy and where it is not.

**What I designed:** a hub that opens with the numbers that matter, such as meetings ahead, open tasks, the hygiene score and capability building. Beneath them are AI executive insights with a confidence label and a field operations heat map. Each cell is one entity at the chosen level, coloured by status, and a person can move between levels, scope the view, and filter to critical areas. Operational status, performance over time, business dimensions and product discussion coverage follow.

<Figure src="/work/salespulze/screens/figma/01-intel-hub.webp" size="full" alt="The Intel Hub with KPI cards, AI executive insights, a field operations health heat map and performance charts" caption="The hub: key numbers, AI insights with a confidence label, and a health map of the field." zoom />

</SubSection>

<SubSection n="02" title="Meeting Repository: one series, understood">

**The problem it solves:** a meeting series is judged by memory.

**What I designed:** a page for each meeting series with an overview, attendance, meetings, action items and blockers. It shows execution health over time, the positive and negative drivers behind the score, a calendar and a prep pack for the next meeting with a focus note. AI-inferred and confidence badges sit beside the title, and a person can flag the series if something looks wrong.

### Before and with SalesPulze

This comparison shows what the design is built to change. These are design intentions, not measured results.

| Dimension | Before | With SalesPulze |
|---|---|---|
| Seeing patterns in meetings | Meetings held and forgotten | Execution health over time and the drivers behind it |

### What I would measure

How often managers open the hub each week; the share of meeting series with a hygiene score above target; the share of AI outputs reviewed or flagged; action items completed on time; and the cost of meetings against budget.

### Reflections and learnings

The hardest part was not any single screen. It was keeping one product honest across five levels of the same organisation, and making sure a number on a managing director's screen could be traced to a manager's meeting.

The other lesson was about trust. People will forgive an AI that is sometimes wrong if it shows how sure it is, where it got its answer, and how to correct it. That is what the confidence label, the evidence and the flag are for.
